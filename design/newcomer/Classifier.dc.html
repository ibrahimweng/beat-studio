Reading the shape, not just the spike
The app already measures how much the picture changes and marks every moment that stands out. It uses only the height of each spike. Reading the shape of the curve around a spike tells you what kind of moment it is, and that is enough to propose a sound and say why.

What goes there
A cut
A short dry impact. Nothing leads into it, so nothing should lead into the sound either.
A build to a hit
A riser that ends on the hit, and an impact on it. Getting this backwards is the first mistake everyone makes.
A move
One whoosh across the whole move. Not three hits inside it, which is what a beginner places.
Something lands and settles
A thud with a sub under it and a tail that decays with the picture. The sub is what makes it felt on a phone.
A quick sequence
Ticks, one per spike, each a little quieter than the last. One sound repeated, not four decisions.
A quiet stretch
Nothing, or a bed. Saying nothing goes here is a suggestion too, and it is the one nobody new to this believes.
Each row in the Moments panel carries the sentence from this table. That sentence is the whole teaching plan. It is read at the moment it applies, about the frame in front of them, and it costs nothing to skip once it stops being needed.
